For `actorConfigLookup` in src/global/8F050.c (fdc560 kept; ledger-only 546781fa, base a04efaed), `uopt -l 6` shows that only the candidate bit order matters: `actorType` must be numbered before `next`'s ustr at the loop top. Statements in one basic block, such as `next = i + 1; *outIndex = next;`, form one expression tree. That makes `i + 1` its own 2-use candidate (uadd bit 24 on `a3`), and the cfg base register moves. If the else arm has `i = next`, the same block becomes `i = i + 1`, and IV elimination strength-reduces `cfg[i]` into `lh t6,0(t0); addiu t0,t0,20` (full=11). Making `i` a `u16` blocks this, but it adds an `andi` (full=6). Every compare-first body tried ends up trading the dead test for either IV elimination or the `i+1` candidate. This extends the cross-block candidate rule (Q0510) and first-appearance colouring (Q0297). Unit "2026-11-15: cleanup HackactorConfigLookup2".

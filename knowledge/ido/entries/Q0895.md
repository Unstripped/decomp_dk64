In `vsprintf` (Hackvsprintf2, merge 60fcb232, e-arm 69d5aa/e415e5/7dcd26), a coupled dead-code steer works by adding exactly one basic block to two competing ranges at once. `exp` (savings 540) grows from 98 to 99 blocks and its adjsave drops from 5.51 to 5.45. The CSE'd `prec > 0` (`ugeq prec,1`, savings 150) grows from 27 to 28 blocks and drops from 5.56 to 5.36. The shorter range loses more priority, so the order flips: `exp` keeps `s3` (0x9c4 `move s3,zero`) and `prec > 0` gets `s4` (0xa1c). The divisor is the `compute_save` divisor from Q0163/Q0523. If you remove the steer pieces one at a time, the effect stays hidden (the diffs are 11, 42 and 154 lines), so it looks like the same coupled knot as Q0602. Read the block counts and adjsave for each range instead of diffing one piece at a time. You can also raise priority by shortening a range (Q0766).

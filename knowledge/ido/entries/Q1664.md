This extends Q1644. An in-place clamp followed by a copy at the join (`bgez v1,L; move v1,zero; L: move s2,v1`) only comes out of TWO variables: the clamped value in a caller-saved register ending at the join, and the value used across later calls in a callee-saved one. A single variable clamped in place and then used across calls is one uopt range coloured into the callee-saved register from its definition: `subu s2,...` and `move s2,zero` in the arm, no join copy.

csCmdShowRemainingDigits: the target keeps v in v1 (`subu v1,t7,t8` 0x69d8, `move v1,zero` 0x69e4) and the digit value in s2 (`move s2,v1` 0x69e8; uopt trace cand 9 v -> v1, cand 23 t -> s2). Every alias-free spelling scores 5: plain if, assignment in the if condition, the in-place ternary `v = (v < 0) ? 0 : v`, a named digit local, the clamp and loop on t. A two-step subtraction scores 21 and do/while loop forms 7. The in-place ternary scores like the plain if; Q1644's 105 applies only to forms that store the ternary straight into the destination.

So the shape Q1644 describes here needs the second variable. When the second variable is a plain `t = v` copy, it is the redundant-alias steer and stays FAKE. `move s2,v1` is register numbering, not ORIG evidence.

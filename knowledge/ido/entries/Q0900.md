In `eepromSaveRecords` (global/104F0.c, ledger-only c5d9ef70, base 40198b93, unit "2026-11-15: cleanup HackeepromSaveRecords2", evidence 6b7e71/081ad7), the uopt candidate numbers for addresses and constants follow their first appearance in the ucode, which is the post-order of leaves in the first statement that uses each one: 176 `&gContReadPending`, 177 const 2, 178 `&D_807EDEAC`, 179 `&D_807ECEA0`, 180 -64, 181 -8, 182 `&sEepromSlotBuffer`. Candidates 179 and 181 tie exactly at adjsave 2.6 over the same 15 blocks. On an exact tie the lower candidate number wins, so 179 takes s4. Adding two dead `if`s past `bzero` adds one block to 179's range, which drops it to 2.333. Candidate 181 then wins s4, as in the target (0x1b28/0x1b38). Practical point: with an exact tie, any epsilon change or any reordering of first appearance flips the colouring. This finding belongs with the 17B90 bit-order finding and should be merged with it.

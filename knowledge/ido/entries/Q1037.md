`(*(p + i)).f`, `i[p].f` and `(&p[i])->f` behave like `p[i].f`: the field operand goes first whichever side it is written on. Only `->` on a non-`&` pointer expression (`(p + i)->f` or a pointer local) keeps the written order (target `add.d f10,f4,f8` at 0xa84 = product + field). Count frame words (Q0937) before trying a pointer local. Refines Q0678.

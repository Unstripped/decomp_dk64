the single most productive lever (matched ~12 near-misses): a callee declared `s32` that is really `void` (or the reverse), `u8`/`s16`/`u16` declared `s32`, or a phantom/missing parameter, changes the caller's `$v0`/`$v1` choice, reserves a hidden call-result word (narrow callee nested as an array index), adds `cvtl` chains that as1 deletes but that consume ring numbers, or forces a `jalr` cast. Check every callee against its definition in all of `src/` first; a function ending in a call to a pointer-returning callee may itself `return` that value. The reverse also holds: a caller that ends in `return f(...)` emits the same `jal`/`jr` as a `void` caller that calls `f` and returns nothing, because `$v0` passes through unchanged. So a local `s32` declaration of a callee that is really `void` that exists only to make such a `return` compile is a steer you can remove: make the caller `void` when nothing reads its result (`func_806018D4` → `__n_alCSeqNextDelta`).

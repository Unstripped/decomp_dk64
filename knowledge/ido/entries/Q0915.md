In `csCmdExec` (src/global/42630.c, cleanup unit 2026-11-15, 7acbc2 x3, kept) the target sequence `move a2,v0` / `andi t5,a2,0x7f` / `move a2,t5` at 0x2fd4-0x2ffc, matched by the double mask `c &= 0xFF; c &= 0x7F` from Q0186, is matched just as well when the redundant byte truncation is written as a cast instead: `(u8)(cmd->c & 0x7F)` or `(u8)cmd->c & 0x7F`. The mechanism is the same. A redundant truncation stops uopt from copy-propagating the CSE'd load into a single `andi`, so the copy into the argument register survives. It is still a hidden steer, so the cast forms were not adopted and the double-mask spelling was kept. Every natural spelling tried (different widths, a single mask, or the mask applied inside the argument expression) scored 253-606 and never produced the copy. So some redundant truncation is needed for this shape; the C spelling of that truncation does not matter.
